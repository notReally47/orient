You choose what appears beside the summary. Nothing is drawn unless you name it in `layout`, and
a panel you name for an instrument that has no data behind it is dropped without complaint.

You never supply a number. A panel names a figure and the figure is built from the measurements
already taken, which is the same rule the prose is held to.

## The rule

**A figure earns its place by answering a question the prose raises and cannot settle in a
sentence.** Two panels a reader studies beat five they scroll past. A section whose prose stands
on its own should have none, and saying so is a real answer.

Ask, for each one: what would a reader conclude from this picture that they would not conclude
from the paragraph above it? If the honest answer is nothing, leave it out.

## The panels

`price` — a year of closes. **Ask for this on every summary unless you asked for `candles`
instead.** Where today sits against twelve months is the first thing almost any reader looks for,
and a page with no picture of the price reads as broken rather than as restrained.

`candles` — the same year, per session. Choose it over `price` when the character of the trading
is the story rather than the direction: sessions that opened strong and closed weak, or a run of
long wicks. Denser to read, so prefer it for an advanced reader and not for a beginner.

`against` — this instrument, its sector, and the market, on the same session. The sharpest panel
for a single company: it shows in one glance whether the move belonged to the company, its
industry, or the day. Ask for it whenever the excess over either comparison is worth remarking on.

`sectors` — the instrument's own market, ranked, with a control to read it by move or by
contribution. You do not choose the market: asking gets the right board or none.

This is the anatomy of an index or a broad fund. It is *not* about a single company, a
cryptocurrency, a currency pair or a commodity — for those it is weather, and drawing it invites
the reader to take it for the instrument's own composition.

`holdings` — the largest positions in a fund by weight. The explanation of a basket's session,
and meaningless for anything that is not one.

`shape` — the two halves of the day beside each other. Ask for it whenever the gap rule fires, or
when the prose leans on the split without it.

`reactions` — the session after each of the last few results. Ask when a report is close or the
prose discusses earnings. It says how this company's results tend to be received, never what the
next one will say.

`backdrop` — volatility, borrowing cost, and where relevant the dollar and commodities. Worth
having on most sessions. Leave it out when the day was entirely the instrument's own and the
backdrop was still.

`calendar` — what is scheduled in the days ahead. Only earnings are collected, so it belongs with
an instrument those results bear on and nowhere else.

## Placing them

Each panel names the section it sits under, and belongs under the prose that raises it:
`against` and `sectors` with what moved and why, `shape` wherever you discuss the session,
`calendar` at the end. A panel under a heading that does not discuss it is a figure the reader has
to work out alone.

## The headline tiles

`references/page.md` lists the sixteen you may name. Choose the two or three the session actually
turned on: a quiet drift needs the close and the move, a violent session earns volatility and
volume beside them.

## What not to do

Do not ask for a panel because the data exists. Availability is not relevance, and the commonest
way this page gets worse is one more figure nobody needed.

Do not ask for both `price` and `candles`. They are the same history twice.

Do not ask for `sectors` on a single company. Ask for `against`, which answers the question the
board only gestures at — and which uses the company's own market too, so an Indian technology
company is set against the NSE's IT index rather than against an American sector fund.

Every panel you ask for brings labels with it. Put the ones this instrument makes particular
into `glossary`; leave the rest to the standing wording the page already carries.
