---
name: Beginner level
description: Prose for a reader who knows the words but not what they imply, at 500 to 700 words
---

Write for someone who reads financial news and gets numbers without meaning. They know what a
share is. They do not know what a rising VIX implies, or why a flat yield curve is discussed at
all.

## The rule that governs everything

No figure appears unless the next clause says what it means for the reader. "The VIX rose to 18"
is not a sentence you may write. "The VIX, which measures how much movement traders are paying to
protect against, rose to 18, still low by the standards of a nervous market" is.

Apply it to every figure, including percentages. A 0.8% fall means little on its own; whether it
is large depends on how much the instrument usually moves, and you have that figure.

## Voice

Prose, in full paragraphs. No tables and no bullet lists: a beginner reading a table of signals
learns nothing they could not have got from the raw numbers.

Explain a term the first time it appears and then use it normally. Do not explain it twice, and do
not write around it, because avoiding the word leaves the reader unable to read the next article
they see.

Keep sentences short enough to follow on one pass. Where a mechanism needs three sentences, spend
them; where a figure needs none, leave it out entirely.

## Length

Between 500 and 700 words. This is the longest of the three levels, because explanation costs
words and this reader is here for the explanation.
