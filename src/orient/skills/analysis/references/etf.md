An ETF has no earnings and no company news, so its session is explained by what it holds.

The profile carries the holdings and the sector weights. A fund that is 30% one sector moves with
that sector, and saying so is usually the whole explanation. `relative` measures it against the
broad market and carries no sector peer: an excess near zero means a tracker did what it exists to
do, and a large one means it is concentrated somewhere the market was not.

Treat a broad fund as an index: the cross-asset block and the sector board carry the day. For a
narrow or thematic fund the concentration is the story, and if one large holding clearly drove it,
a news search on that name earns its round trip.

Do not write about fees, tracking error or fund structure. Nothing measured them.
