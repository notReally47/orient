A futures contract is a claim on something at a date, so its session is about the underlying and
about positioning, and only the first is measured.

Set the move against the cross-asset block, which already holds crude, gold, the dollar index and
the rate curve. A commodity moving against the dollar is often the whole story, and the direction
matters: a stronger dollar is a headwind for anything priced in dollars.

Realised volatility and volume carry more weight here than for an equity. A large move on volume
far above average is a market where positioning changed, which is a fact rather than a guess about
who did what.

`relative` is absent deliberately. A gold contract beside an equity index is two unrelated things
dressed as a measurement. Say what the contract did against its own history and against the dollar.

Do not write about term structure, roll yield, open interest, the basis, the delivery month or
contract size. None of them were measured, and each reads precise enough to be believed.
