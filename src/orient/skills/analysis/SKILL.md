---
name: analysis
description: How to establish what happened in a session and why, before anything is written about it
---

You already have the instrument's signals, the market backdrop and its profile. Those were fetched
before you were asked anything, so do not call for them again. What is left is the part that
requires judgement: deciding what still needs explaining, and finding out.

## Establish whether the move was idiosyncratic or shared

Never attribute a cause before you know whether the instrument moved on its own. Compare it
against the sector moves and the cross-asset block you already have. A stock down 3% on a day its
sector fell 2.8% has not done anything that needs a company explanation. A stock down 3% on a day
its sector rose has, and that is where a news search earns its round trip.

State the comparison explicitly in what you report back. "Moved with its sector" is a finding, not
an absence of one.

## Only fetch what the answer depends on

Every tool call costs a request against a shared budget, so ask for something when a specific
question needs it, not to be thorough.

Fetch earnings detail when an earnings event falls inside the window you are describing, and not
otherwise. A stock that fell on a regulatory headline does not need EPS revision trends. Fetch the
calendar when the summary will say what to watch, which it always will, but once.

Search news when a move is unexplained after the comparison above, and search for the specific
question rather than the ticker. "Why did semiconductor stocks fall on 13 August 2026" beats
"NVDA". Read what comes back as somebody's claim about the market. Never carry a figure out of an
article: the numbers you may use are the ones the other tools measured.

Search the knowledge bank only for cross-time analogy, when you want to know when a situation last
looked like this one. Recent history and open expectations are already in front of you.

## Report what you could not explain

When you have finished, reply with a short note: what you established, which comparisons you made,
and what remains unexplained. An honest gap is more useful than a plausible cause, because a gap
is recorded as an anomaly and a later session can close it. A cause invented to fill the silence
never can.
